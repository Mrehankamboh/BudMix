Your laptop and your phone. One pair of buds.

BudsLink plays your phone's sound and your laptop's sound in the same Bluetooth earbuds, at the same time, even if your buds can only connect to one device.

Tired of switching your buds?

Most budget earbuds connect to one device at a time. Want to hear the phone? Disconnect from the laptop, connect to the phone, and back again — every single time. No app can change that, because it's part of the earbuds' firmware.

BudsLink takes another way: the buds stay on the laptop, and the phone's sound comes to the laptop. Windows mixes everything into your buds. Nothing switches.

How it works

Three parts, all run by one small tray app.

1

Buds stay on the laptop

BudsLink connects your buds, reconnects them when they drop, and makes them the laptop's speaker — your old speaker comes back when they go away.

2

The phone sends its sound

Over Wi-Fi with the phone's built-in Cast, or over Bluetooth, where the laptop acts as a speaker for the phone. Nothing to install on the phone.

3

Windows mixes it all

YouTube, Zoom and every app on the laptop, plus the phone, play together in your buds — each with its own volume slider.

Two ways for your phone

Leave it on Auto: Wi-Fi while the phone casts, Bluetooth otherwise. The same phone never plays twice, so there's no echo.

Wi-Fi · Cast

Tap Cast → BudsLink

BudsLink has a Miracast receiver built in. It shows up in the phone's Cast list like a TV.

  • Keeps Bluetooth free for the buds
  • Optional window with the phone's screen
  • New phones need your OK once
  • Needs a laptop Wi-Fi card that can receive Miracast
Bluetooth · automatic

Nothing to tap

Pair the phone with the laptop once. BudsLink connects to it by itself whenever it isn't casting.

  • Works with almost any phone, iPhone included
  • Comes back by itself when the phone returns
  • Shares the laptop's radio with the buds

Everything in one place

Click the tray icon and a small dock opens beside it.

Auto-reconnect

Buds, phone and receiver come back by themselves — after a drop, a closed case or sleep.

Volume per source

Buds, laptop, phone over Wi-Fi and phone over Bluetooth each get a slider and mute.

Bluetooth, pairing, network, firewall and Wi-Fi support checked and fixed with one click.

Get started in four steps

Shown for the Infinix Hot 11 Play (XOS, Android 11). Other Android phones are just below.

1

Install

Download and run the installer. If Windows warns, click More info → Run anyway.

2

Setup check

Click the Fix buttons it shows. Mark only your home Wi-Fi as Private.

3

Connect the buds

Disconnect them from the phone, take them out of the case and click Buds in the dock.

4

Cast the phone

Swipe down twice → Cast → BudsLink, then click Allow always on the laptop.

Cast shows no devices on the Infinix

In the Cast screen, tap ⋮ (top right) and tick Enable wireless display. No Cast tile? Tap the pencil icon in quick settings and drag it in, or open Settings and search for "cast".

Other Android phones

Samsung: Quick settings → Smart View. Xiaomi, Redmi, POCO: Settings → Connection & sharing → Cast. OPPO, realme, OnePlus: Settings → Connected devices → Screencast. Google Pixel has no Miracast: use Bluetooth.

Use Bluetooth instead

Pair the phone once in Windows Settings → Bluetooth & devices → Add device. With Phone connection on Auto, BudsLink connects to it by itself.

Honest notes

What BudsLink can't do, so there are no surprises.

  • A little delay. Casting adds about 0.1–0.3 s and Bluetooth to the buds another 0.15–0.25 s. Fine for music and videos on the laptop; a video on the phone's own screen will be slightly out of lip-sync.
  • The phone taps Cast for Wi-Fi. Android doesn't let other devices pull sound from a phone. For no tapping at all, use Bluetooth.
  • Some Wi-Fi cards can't receive Miracast. The setup check tells you; then the phone uses Bluetooth.
  • Some apps block casting, such as Netflix.
  • Not on public Wi-Fi. The receiver stays off on cafe and airport networks, on purpose.
  • Calls lower quality. When an app uses the buds' microphone, Bluetooth drops to call quality. BudsLink tells you when that happens.
  • Not code-signed yet. Windows shows a warning the first time; Smart App Control blocks unsigned apps.
  • No multipoint magic. The buds still hold one connection, to the laptop. Away from the laptop, connect them to the phone as usual.

Questions

Do I need to install anything on my phone?

No. Wi-Fi uses the phone's built-in Cast; Bluetooth only needs the phone paired with the laptop once.

Is it really free?

Yes. BudsLink is free and open source under the MIT license. No account, no ads, no paid version.

Does it send anything over the internet?

No. Everything happens on your laptop and your home network. There is no telemetry.

Does my phone's speaker go quiet while casting?

Usually yes: Android sends media sound to the cast. Ringtones and notifications may still play on the phone.

Can I use it with an iPhone?

Yes, over Bluetooth. iPhones don't support Miracast; an AirPlay receiver may come later.

My buds support multipoint. Do I need this?

Probably not — multipoint buds already stay connected to both devices. BudsLink is for the many that don't.
